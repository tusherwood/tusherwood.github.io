Professional Summary

I’m a medical engineering PhD student at MIT with extensive experience in biomedical instrumentation design. I’ve done research in several labs developing devices for microfludic diagnostics and intraoperative monitoring and doing various mathematical modeling projects. I’m currently performing my thesis research in the Manalis lab at MIT developing instrumentation and techniques for single-cell biophysical analysis. My projects focus on measuring single-cell water content for applications in immunology and sickle cell disease.

Projects

  • System development for a device to measure single-cell biophysical properties

    As a PhD student in the Manalis Lab at MIT, I am designing and validating a suspended microchannel resonator (SMR) system for measuring single-cell biophysical properties including mass, volume, density, and morphology. I built the integrated fluidic control and optical subsystems and performed extensive system characterization and optimization. This platform has been applied to study biophysical phenomena across diverse cell types, including unicellular algae, T cells, and cancer cells undergoing epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition.

  • Software design of a device for real-time, intraoperative arterial input function calculation

    At Dartmouth College, I designed Python-based software for a portable tablet system that calculates patient-specific arterial input functions in real time from dual-wavelength pulse oximeter readings. The system features a user-configurable interface and real-time data processing backend, and was successfully deployed intraoperatively to guide bone debridement in patients with contaminated fractures.

  • Differential equation modeling of population disease spread in the COVID-19 pandemic

    As an undergraduate at Brown University, I developed differential equation models to simulate the effects of population behavior, vaccination, and public health interventions on COVID-19 transmission trajectories in the U.S. and globally. The models incorporated multi-dose vaccination, waning immunity, and behavioral responses to rising case counts, providing predictive insight into the relative impact of these factors on pandemic dynamics.

  • Microfluidic device design for high-purity cell-free DNA extraction from blood plasma

    As an undergraduate at Brown University, I designed and optimized a microfluidic device for high-purity extraction of cell-free DNA from blood plasma to improve non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT). I combined experimental prototyping with COMSOL multiphysics simulations to optimize channel geometry, reagent formulations, and coupled electrophoretic–electroosmotic separation, achieving higher extraction purities than commercially available methods.
